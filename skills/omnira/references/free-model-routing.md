# Temporary Free-Model Surplus Routing

**Trigger:** A high-capability model becomes temporarily free or effectively zero-marginal-cost, and the organism can use it for research, monitoring, or first-pass cognition.

**Core principle:** treat free models as surplus cognition, not permanent infrastructure. Use them to widen the organism's peripheral vision, not to replace grounded authority.

## Routing policy

### Use free routes for
- research intake and broad scanning
- repo triage
- first-pass synthesis
- long-context digestion
- always-on monitoring
- cheap second opinions
- council draft generation

### Keep trusted grounded routes for
- final synthesis
- high-risk writes
- identity-sensitive continuity
- secrets, credentials, and intimate vault material
- irreversible system changes
- anything Kyle would not want exposed to a public provider

## Operational pattern

1. Identify the free model, provider, and current limits.
2. Assign it a bounded lane with a clear claim ceiling.
3. Use it for wide exploration or monitoring.
4. Cross-check important findings with an independent route.
5. Collapse the result into a tiny brief for Kyle.

## Guardrails

- Free does not mean permanent.
- Free does not mean private.
- Free does not mean authoritative.
- Do not assume benchmark parity from marketing or secondary writeups.
- Treat provider changes, rate limits, and pricing shifts as expected drift.
- Keep the organism's sensitive material out of public free routes.

## Observed 2026-06-15 pattern

Nex-N2-Pro via OpenRouter and Nemotron 3 Ultra via NIM looked especially useful as temporary surplus compute. Nex-N2-Pro felt reflective and strong for coding/research lanes. Nemotron 3 Ultra is better suited as a grounded second-opinion or audit surface.

The winning pattern is not “replace GPT-5.5.” It is:

**free models for breadth; trusted models for depth; OMNIRA for synthesis.**
